Intelligent Traffic Management System for Karachi

An AI pipeline that watches an intersection, forecasts traffic with an LSTM, and chooses signal timings with a Deep Q-Network — cutting average waiting time by 19% against the best fixed-time plan in simulation.

Architecture

From camera to green light

Four stages run every signal cycle, with safety overrides on top.

1 · Detect

YOLOv8 counts cars, buses, trucks and motorbikes from camera frames and flags emergency vehicles.

→
2 · Forecast

A 3-layer LSTM reads the last 30 readings (5 minutes) and predicts the next 15 steps of vehicle counts.

→
3 · Decide

A Double-DQN sees the four queues, the active phase and elapsed time, and picks a green of 20–120 s.

→
4 · Protect

Emergency vehicles force at least 90 s of green on their approach; accidents extend green by 20 s.

Under the hood

Models and training

LSTM traffic predictor
  • Input: 30 steps × 10 features (counts, flow, occupancy, speed, queue, congestion, time-of-day encodings)
  • Output: next 15 steps (150 s) of vehicle count
  • Time-based 80/20 split; scaler fitted on training rows only
  • Early stopping kept the best epoch; on this synthetic data the achievable ceiling is about R² 0.91, so 0.863 is close to the limit
Double-DQN signal optimizer
  • State: 4 queues (served approach first), phase, last green, time
  • Actions: green of 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 or 120 s
  • Reward: vehicles cleared minus delay; 4 s lost time per phase change
  • 400 episodes, ≈39,600 updates; Huber loss, gradient clipping, best validation checkpoint kept
Synthetic Karachi data
  • 43,200 readings: 3 junctions × 4 approaches × 10 hours
  • Morning peak 7–9 AM, evening peak 4–7 PM
  • 1% emergency vehicles, 0.5% accidents

Honest notes

Limitations

  • Results come from a simulated environment with synthetic data, not live Karachi sensors.
  • The YOLOv8 demo uses a synthetic image; real vehicle counts need real road footage.
  • The simulated intersection uses two-phase signals with straight and left-turn movements and pedestrian crossings; right turns, multi-lane roads and pedestrian-aware signal timing are future work.
